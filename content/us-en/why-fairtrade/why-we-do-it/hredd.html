Fairtrade embeds human rights and environmental due diligence

Fairtrade works to support farmers and workers, foster responsible business conduct and induce policy reforms that protect human and environmental rights – in order to mitigate human rights risks in global supply chains.

Poverty and inequality are the root causes of many risks in global supply chains. That’s why collaboration of suppliers, buyers, governments and civil society is important. In order for farmers to invest in strong due diligence measures, they need to achieve better prices, longer lasting business relationships, and co-investment among all supply chain actors.

Human Rights and Environmental Due Diligence (HREDD) is a company’s responsibility and means managing the social and environmental risks and harms linked to its business. If conducted properly HREDD can be groundbreaking in advancing farmers’ and workers’ rights and environmental sustainability.

For Fairtrade, the most important issues around human rights and environmental due diligence in global supply chains include:

  • A living wage and living income
  • Preventing child labour and forced labour 
  • Preventing discrimination on the basis of gender, ethnic origin or other status 
  • Decent working conditions
  • Environmental rights
  • Freedom of association and unionisation

How Fairtrade supports Human Rights and Environmental Due Diligence

Fairtrade Standards

We are currently strengthening the due diligence requirements for all Fairtrade certified organisations – traders and processors, plantations, farmer cooperatives and contract production settings.

Support to producers

Fairtrade offers ongoing advice, training and programme-based support to Fairtrade farmers, workers, miners and management at plantations and factories. One of our tools for this is the Fairtrade Risk Map.

Funding

We channel funds to producers through Fairtrade Minimum Prices, Fairtrade Premium and specific programmes to strengthen the producers’ position and due diligence measures.

Business services

Corporate due diligence for human rights and the environment always remains a duty of care for companies. Fairtrade can assist in order achieve the best results for people on the ground as well as the environment. In particular, that includes risk prevention, risk mitigation, and meaningful engagement between companies and people on the ground.

How Fairtrade supports the OECD Due Dililgence Guidance

Embed responsible business conduct into policies & management systems:

Understanding of human rights and the duty of care process, the challenges specific to raw materials and regions, and their root causes.

Identify & assess adverse impacts:

Information and opportunities for dialogue between farmers, workers and other actors in the sector.

Cease, prevent or mitigate adverse impacts:

Support, advice and requirements for agricultural operations and actors in the sector in order to prevent and mitigate negative impacts on human rights and the environment.

Track implementation and results:

Monitoring of corrective measures; data from ongoing research and questionnaires intended for producers; aggregated audit data.

Communicate how impacts are addressed:

Data, analysis, examples and photos of results.

Provide for or cooperate in remediation when appropriate:

Support for remediation of child labour, forced labour and gender-based violence.
