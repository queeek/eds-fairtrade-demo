Fairtrade is good for producers

Producers are at the heart of Fairtrade. Investing in a Fairtrade certification can give your producer organisation greater economic stability and help you to become more sustainable.

Volatile global market prices, power imbalances in supply chains and the consequences of climate change are tough challenges for farmers and workers in Latin America, the Caribbean, Africa, the Middle East, Asia, and the Pacific region.

Often too many trade risks and responsibilities are pushed onto these producers, making it difficult to achieve a price that pays for production and the labour going into it. New rules about respecting human rights within supply chains (HRDD) are coming into effect in several regions of the world.

Without fairer trade agreements, producers are most likely left to pay the price for such important ethical regulations. Meanwhile crops are threatened by rising temperatures, floods and other effects of the changing climate, again increasing the pressure on producers.

Under such challenging circumstances, Fairtrade aims to strengthen producers and help them to build resilience.

Why join Fairtrade?

Less volatile prices

There is a Fairtrade Minimum Price for most certifiable products. It provides producers with a safety net against falling market prices for their sold produce and allows for long-term planning.

Additional Premium

With the Fairtrade Premium, producers can improve the quality of their lives, communities, and businesses. It is an additional amount of money paid on top of the agreed Fairtrade price for sold certified goods, which producers decide democratically how to use. The Fairtrade Premium is often invested in education, healthcare, and farm improvements to increase income.

Have your say

Fairtrade certified farmers and workers co-own and manage Fairtrade International – through elected delegates participating in the board of directors, key committees, and consultation processes, producers can influence Fairtrade’s strategy, standards, prices and premiums.

Strong organisations

With Fairtrade, small-scale farmer groups have a democratic structure and transparent administration. In plantations and hired labour settings, workers form a committee that decides on the use of the Fairtrade Premium. Fairtrade offers support to achieve this and strengthens producer organisations in the process.

Market opportunities

Fairtrade is the most recognised ethical label in the world. Fairtrade’s global network of marketing organisations can extend producers' reach to connect with businesses seeking sustainably sourced products under a label they trust.

Power of community

Producer organizations can join one of the three regional Fairtrade Producer Networks which provide support, training and representation for producers in the global Fairtrade system. They represent small-scale producers, workers, and other producer stakeholders. Please visit their websites for further information.

Long lasting change

Fairtrade encourages positive change through time. The standards require producer organisations to define medium and long term goals and helps them to follow up on them.

Living incomes, living wages, gender equality, opportunities for young people and adaptation to climate change are areas Fairtrade offers support and hosts special programmes for.

We support the next generation of farmers to carry forward values of fairness and sustainability, while developing skills in leadership, management, advocacy, and business development that will serve them well as farmers, cooperative managers, and community leaders.

More than a label

We don’t just label products as ethical—we create real sustainable change by more equitably distributing resources to the beginning of the supply chain, leveraging local and regional agricultural expertise and creating standards that protect people and the environment.
